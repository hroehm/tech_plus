AAA Authentication

Explore how systems securely confirm identity, control access, and track user actions using the fundamental frameworks of digital security.

The AAA Triad

  • Authentication: Proving exactly who you are to the system.
  • Authorization: Controlling what actions you are permitted to perform.
  • Accounting & Non-repudiation: Tracking your actions so they cannot be denied later.

Single-Factor (SFA)

  • One Method: Relies on only a single form of verification to grant access.
  • Common Types: Usually a password, a PIN, or a standalone biometric scan.
  • Vulnerability: Highly susceptible to theft, guessing, and automated brute-force attacks.

Multi-Factor (MFA)

  • Defense in Depth: Requires two or more entirely different categories of factors.
  • The Factors: Something you Know, something you Have, and something you Are.
  • Enhanced Security: Attackers must simultaneously breach multiple independent security layers.

Single Sign-On (SSO)

  • One Login: A single authentication grants access to multiple interconnected applications.
  • User Experience: Drastically reduces password fatigue and lost credential issues.
  • The Master Key Risk: If the SSO token is compromised, the attacker accesses everything.
