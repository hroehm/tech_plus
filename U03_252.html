Peripheral Devices & Interfaces

Explore how computers connect with input, output, and external storage devices through wired standards like USB and Thunderbolt, as well as wireless frequencies like Bluetooth and RF.

USB (A/B/C Standards)

  • Universal Standard: Standardized interface used for data transfer and/or power supply across systems.
  • USB-A: Most common rectangular connector for keyboards, mice, and flash drives.
  • USB-B: Square connector used primarily for printers and older scanners.
  • USB-C: Modern, reversible, high-speed universal connector for modern laptops and phones.

Thunderbolt Interface

  • High Speed: Advanced interface supporting data transfer rates up to 40 Gbps.
  • Multi-Function: Supports simultaneous data transfer, 4K/8K video output, and laptop charging over one USB-C form factor cable.
  • Daisy Chaining: Connects multiple displays and external storage arrays in series through a single port.

Bluetooth Wireless

  • Short-Range: Wireless technology designed for connecting peripherals over relatively short distances.
  • Low Power: Highly optimized for battery-operated accessories like wireless mice, keyboards, and headphones.
  • Compatibility: Built natively into almost all modern smartphones, tablets, and laptops.

Radio Frequency (RF)

  • Wave Communication: Uses dedicated radio waves to communicate with specialized USB dongles.
  • Low Latency: Provides near-wired performance without lag, ideal for competitive gaming mice and keyboards.
  • Broad Spectrum: Encompasses Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and NFC while operating on discrete frequencies to minimize interference.

Apple Lightning

  • Proprietary Standard: Specialized 8-pin connector developed exclusively by Apple for iOS devices.
  • Reversible Design: Small, symmetrical connector that can be inserted in either orientation.
  • Ecosystem Transition: Widely used on older iPhones and iPads before regulatory shifts toward universal USB-C.
