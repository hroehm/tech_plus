Peripheral Devices & Interfaces

Master the wired and wireless connections that empower computers to communicate with external hardware. Review this briefing before moving to the Lab Activity.

Peripheral Basics

  • Purpose: Peripheral interfaces are connection points that allow computers to communicate with external devices.
  • Functionality: They provide vital pathways for both data transfer and power supply.
  • Standardization: They define a common way for devices to connect, minimizing compatibility issues across different hardware brands.

Universal Serial Bus (USB)

  • USB-A: The most common, older rectangular connector. Primarily used for standard peripherals like keyboards and mice.
  • USB-B: A square-shaped connector with beveled corners, typically used for printers and scanners.
  • USB-C: The modern, versatile, and reversible connector. Supports high data transfer rates and higher power for charging devices.

High-Speed & Proprietary

  • Thunderbolt: A high-speed interface that utilizes the USB-C physical connector. Supports massive data transfer rates (up to 40 Gbps), video output, and power delivery simultaneously.
  • Lightning: A proprietary, reversible connector developed strictly by Apple for older iPhones and iPads. Supports data transfer and charging.

Wireless Interfaces

  • Bluetooth: A short-range wireless technology that is designed for low power consumption, making it highly compatible with battery-operated devices (headphones, mice).
  • Radio Frequency (RF): A broader category that uses specific radio waves to communicate. Often used with a dedicated USB dongle to provide very low-latency connections for competitive gaming.
