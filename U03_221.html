Installing Internal Computing Components

Explore the fundamental internal architecture of computer systems, their specific hardware roles, and how they compare to biological systems.

Motherboard

  • Central Hub: Connects all internal components.
  • Form Factors: Proprietary & Standardized layouts.
  • Chipsets: Northbridge (fast) & Southbridge (slow).
  • Expansion Slots: PCIe, M.2, and DIMMs.

BIOS / UEFI

  • Firmware: Embedded startup instructions.
  • POST Routine: Power-On Self-Test checking hardware.
  • Hardware Init: Prepares system for OS handoff.
  • UEFI Upgrade: Modern GUI firmware replacement.

CPU

  • The Brain: Executes instructions and logic.
  • ALU & CU: Arithmetic and Control units.
  • Instruction Cycle: Fetch, decode, execute, store.
  • Sockets: LGA (pads) & PGA (pins).

GPU

  • Visual Processor: Renders UI and 3D graphics.
  • Parallel Processing: Thousands of specialized cores.
  • Pipeline: Vertex, rasterization, shading, output.
  • VRAM: Dedicated high-speed frame buffer.

RAM

  • Short-Term Memory: Fast workspace for CPU.
  • Volatile: Clears data when power shuts off.
  • DIMMs/SODIMM: Desktop and laptop modules.
  • DRAM & DDR: Double data rate memory standards.

Storage

  • Long-Term Memory: Retains data permanently.
  • Non-Volatile: Preserves state without power.
  • Tiers: Secondary (SSD/HDD) & Tertiary (Tape).
  • Metrics: Capacity, performance, reliability.

NIC

  • Network Gateway: Mouth and ears of the PC.
  • Wired vs Wireless: Ethernet cables vs radio waves.
  • Onboard vs Expansion: Integrated vs PCIe cards.
  • Failover: Redundancy for uptime reliability.

Cooling Systems

  • Thermal Control: Prevents heat throttling.
  • Air Cooling: Fans and heat pipes.
  • Liquid Cooling: Pumps and water blocks.
  • Heat Sinks: Metal dissipation fins.
