The Binary Number System

Why do computers only speak in 1s and 0s, and how can we learn their language? Explore the fundamental logic that powers all modern digital technology.

Binary & Hardware

  • Base-2 System: A mathematical system utilizing only two digits: 0 and 1.
  • Transistors: Microscopic electronic switches that represent physical states of On (1) or Off (0).
  • Logic Gates: Circuits that use binary inputs to perform logical operations (AND, OR, NOT).

Bits, Bytes, & Storage

  • The Bit: Short for "binary digit," the absolute smallest unit of digital data.
  • The Byte: A collection of 8 bits, capable of representing 256 unique combinations.
  • Scaling Up: Storage multiplies rapidly into Kilobytes (KB), Megabytes (MB), and Gigabytes (GB).

Binary Conversions

  • Powers of 2: Binary place values double as you move left: 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128...
  • Binary to Decimal: Achieved by multiplying set bits by their place value and adding them together.
  • Decimal to Binary: Achieved by dividing the decimal by 2 and recording the remainders.

Information Coding

  • ASCII: American Standard Code for Information Interchange; maps text characters to binary numbers.
  • Case Sensitivity: Capital 'A' (01000001) and lowercase 'a' (01100001) possess entirely distinct binary values.
  • Unicode: A global expansion of ASCII designed to support characters from every language, plus emojis.
