Internet Service Types

Explore the various broadband internet connections available, how they function physically, and how to choose the right service based on a user's location, speed requirements, and budget.

Wired Broadband (Fiber & Cable)

  • Fiber Optic: Transmits data via light signals through glass/plastic cores. Extremely fast, low latency, symmetrical speeds.
  • Cable Internet: Uses copper coaxial cables and DOCSIS standards. Wide availability, asymmetrical speeds, shared bandwidth.

Telephone & Fixed Wireless (DSL & RF)

  • DSL: Digital Subscriber Line. Runs over existing copper telephone wires. Budget-friendly, highly distance-dependent.
  • RF (Radio Frequency): Fixed wireless using antennas. Requires line-of-sight to a base tower. Great for rural valleys.

Space & Cellular (Satellite & 5G)

  • Satellite: Broadcasts globally from orbit. Excellent for extreme remote areas. Plagued by high latency and weather interference.
  • Cellular: Utilizes mobile network towers (4G/LTE/5G). Perfect for mobility and on-the-go access. Often features data limits.

Crucial Performance Factors

  • Speed vs Latency: Speed dictates how much data flows per second (Bandwidth). Latency dictates the delay before the flow begins (Ping).
  • Considerations: Choosing the right ISP requires balancing Equipment needs, Coverage availability, and Cost/Data limits.
