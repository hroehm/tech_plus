Internet Service Types

Explore how broadband connections transmit data across the globe, and evaluate the equipment, speed, latency, availability, and trade-offs of each connection type.

Fiber Optic

  • Technology: Transmits data as light signals through glass or plastic cores.
  • Speed: 100Mbps up to 25Gbps (Symmetrical).
  • Latency: Extremely low (10ms to 25ms).
  • Pros: Unmatched speed, high bandwidth, immune to electrical interference.

Cable Internet

  • Technology: Uses copper coaxial cables originally built for cable television.
  • Speed: 10Mbps up to 2Gbps (Asymmetrical).
  • Latency: Low to moderate (10ms to 50ms).
  • Pros: Widely available in urban/suburban zones, easy installation.

DSL Internet

  • Technology: Uses existing twisted-pair telephone lines for data transmission.
  • Speed: 5Mbps up to 30Mbps.
  • Latency: Moderate (20ms to 50ms).
  • Pros: Available anywhere there is a landline phone; affordable.

Radio Frequency (RF)

  • Technology: Fixed wireless using rooftop antennas communicating with base station towers.
  • Speed: 1Mbps up to 30Mbps.
  • Latency: Moderate (20ms to 60ms).
  • Pros: Delivers internet to remote rural areas without wired infrastructure.

Satellite Internet

  • Technology: Communicates via orbiting satellites in space and local dishes.
  • Speed: 3Mbps up to 250Mbps.
  • Latency: High (100ms and over due to distance).
  • Pros: Global coverage, perfect for extremely remote or mountainous regions.

Cellular Internet

  • Technology: Transmits data over 4G, LTE, and 5G mobile network towers.
  • Speed: 40Mbps up to 300Mbps.
  • Latency: Low (1ms up to 30ms).
  • Pros: High mobility, works anywhere with cell reception, easy tethering.
