Storage Devices & Memory

Explore how computers permanently store data, the critical differences between memory and storage, and the hardware that makes it all possible.

Memory vs. Storage

  • Memory (RAM): Temporary, high-speed workspace. Volatile (loses data when power is off). Used for active programs.
  • Storage: Long-term data retention. Non-volatile (retains data without power). Used for OS, apps, and files.
  • SATA: The standard interface connecting storage drives to the motherboard, dictating data transfer speeds.

HDDs & Optical Drives

  • HDD (Hard Disk Drive): Uses spinning magnetic platters. Offers high capacity at a low cost, but access speeds are slow.
  • Durability: HDDs are susceptible to physical damage due to moving mechanical parts.
  • Optical Drives: Use laser technology (CD, DVD, Blu-Ray). Becoming obsolete, but excellent for low-cost, long-term archival.

Solid State & NVMe

  • SSD (Solid State Drive): Uses flash memory. No moving parts, making them highly durable, fast, and power-efficient.
  • NVMe: A protocol that allows SSDs to communicate directly with the CPU over the PCIe bus, bypassing SATA bottlenecks.
  • Performance: NVMe SSDs provide the absolute lowest latency and highest transfer speeds available.

Firmware & Portability

  • ROM (Read-Only Memory): Permanent, non-volatile storage holding critical system instructions (like the BIOS/UEFI).
  • External Flash Drives: Portable, non-volatile flash storage. Connects via USB for easy file transfer between devices.
  • Trade-offs: Flash drives are cheap and portable, but offer lower capacities and can be easily lost.
